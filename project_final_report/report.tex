\begin{abstract}
This work compares three generative paradigms---a spatial variational autoencoder (VAE), a pixel-space denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM), and a latent diffusion model (LDM)---on balanced grayscale Fashion-MNIST and an imbalanced 15-class color apparel subset. Each model--dataset pair is trained under five independent seeds, producing 30 trained generative models. Hyperparameters are selected using only training-derived validation sets, and a separately trained residual classifier supplies task-specific features and class diagnostics. Pixel DDPM obtains the lowest feature FID on both datasets: $29.64\pm1.49$ and $16.67\pm0.85$, compared with LDM's $52.01\pm0.71$ and $33.65\pm2.21$, and VAE's $119.10\pm2.87$ and $83.88\pm3.80$. LDM has the highest evaluator confidence and recognizability and samples approximately 13.9 times faster than pixel DDPM, whereas VAE is approximately 292 times faster than DDPM but has the largest distribution error. All models cover every evaluator class and all runs remain numerically finite. The evidence identifies a reproducible quality--compute frontier rather than a universally best model or a parameter-matched causal comparison of objectives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep generative models can represent an image distribution through fundamentally different computational paths. A VAE maps an image to a regularized probability distribution in a compact latent space and produces a sample with one decoder pass \citep{kingma2014vae}. A DDPM instead learns to reverse a gradual Gaussian corruption process and generates an image through many sequential denoising updates \citep{ho2020ddpm}. Latent diffusion combines these ideas: diffusion operates in a learned spatial latent representation, after which a decoder maps the denoised latent to image space \citep{rombach2022ldm}. The three approaches suggest different balances among fidelity, coverage, latency, and memory.

Our central question is: \emph{how do these three systems trade generation quality against computational efficiency, and does the ranking transfer from a simple grayscale dataset to a smaller, imbalanced color dataset?} We address this question using a unified 32$\times$32 RGB interface, a frozen task-specific evaluator, 5,000 generated samples per trained model, and five independent optimization seeds. The comparison extends beyond visual inspection by reporting distribution metrics, recognizability, class balance, feature diversity, sharpness, model size, wall-clock cost, and run-to-run variation.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a shared benchmark of VAE, pixel DDPM, and LDM systems on Fashion-MNIST and a 15-class color apparel subset;
  \item validation-only hyperparameter selection and a stronger residual evaluator whose official test performance is disclosed;
  \item a five-seed protocol comprising 30 generative training runs, with mean and sample standard deviation reported for every principal result; and
  \item an explicit quality--compute analysis that includes batched sampling throughput, training time, parameters, and peak allocated GPU memory.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Problem Formulation}
Let $x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)$ denote a 32$\times$32 RGB apparel image. The goal is to learn a model distribution $p_\theta(x)$ whose samples are visually plausible and whose distribution is close to the held-out data distribution.

\subsection{Variational autoencoder}
The VAE encoder predicts a diagonal Gaussian posterior
\begin{equation}
q_\phi(z\mid x)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(z;\mu_\phi(x),\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x))\right).
\end{equation}
Using the reparameterization $z=\mu_\phi(x)+\sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, the model minimizes the negative evidence lower bound
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VAE}}=
-\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]
+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\right).
\end{equation}
The KL term makes prior sampling possible but can remove fine detail when regularization and compression dominate reconstruction.

\subsection{Pixel-space diffusion}
For the DDPM, the forward process corrupts a clean image at timestep $t$:
\begin{equation}
x_t=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
\end{equation}
A time-conditioned U-Net $\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)$ is trained by noise-prediction mean-squared error,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{simple}}=
\mathbb{E}_{x_0,t,\epsilon}\left[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|_2^2\right].
\end{equation}
We adopt the cosine cumulative noise schedule motivated by improved DDPMs \citep{nichol2021improved}. Sampling follows 100 ancestral reverse steps in pixel space.

\subsection{Latent diffusion}
The LDM applies the same noise-prediction objective to a spatial VAE representation rather than directly to $x$. If $E$ and $D$ are the pretrained encoder and decoder, respectively, we compute $z=E(x)$, standardize every latent channel over the training set, learn the reverse process in the resulting 4$\times$8$\times$8 space, and decode $D(z_0)$. This reduces the denoiser's spatial workload by a factor of 16 and its scalar representation from 3,072 pixels to 256 latent values, while retaining spatial structure. Consistent latent scaling is important because it controls the effective diffusion signal-to-noise ratio \citep{rombach2022ldm}.

\section{Datasets and Preprocessing}
Fashion-MNIST contains 60,000 training and 10,000 test images across ten balanced clothing categories \citep{xiao2017fashion}. Images are resized from 28$\times$28 to 32$\times$32 and the grayscale channel is replicated three times. This keeps the underlying dataset unchanged while matching the RGB interface used by every model.

The second domain is derived from Fashion Product Images Small \citep{fpis}. We retain records whose master category is Apparel, select the 15 most frequent article types, and use a fixed seed to create a class-stratified 85/15 split. The resulting split contains 8,007 training and 1,423 test photographs. Each RGB image is center-padded to a square with a white background before antialiased resizing, avoiding the aspect-ratio distortion caused by direct stretching. The selected labels are T-shirts, shirts, kurtas, tops, briefs, jeans, trousers, shorts, bras, dresses, sarees, track pants, sweaters, sweatshirts, and jackets. The class frequencies are naturally imbalanced; therefore overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, per-class recall, entropy, and class-distribution divergence are all reported.

\section{Model and Training Design}
\subsection{Shared optimization controls}
Every generative model uses training batch size 256, AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}, weight decay $10^{-4}$, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, 200-step linear learning-rate warm-up, and cosine decay to 10\% of the initial rate \citep{loshchilov2017sgdr}. BF16 automatic mixed precision, pinned-memory loaders, four persistent workers, prefetching, and cuDNN benchmarking are enabled on the RTX 5070 Ti. Diffusion inference also uses BF16 and a 4,096-image batch chosen by a local throughput/VRAM probe. A fixed number of optimizer updates is used instead of nominal epochs because the two datasets differ substantially in size; this avoids giving the smaller color dataset drastically fewer parameter updates.

\subsection{Spatial VAE}
The VAE uses residual convolutional blocks with GroupNorm and SiLU activations. Two stride-2 encoder stages map 3$\times$32$\times$32 images to Gaussian moments of shape 4$\times$8$\times$8. A symmetric nearest-neighbor upsampling decoder avoids checkerboard artifacts associated with poorly configured transposed convolutions. Binary reconstruction loss is summed over pixels and averaged over the batch. The selected $\beta=1$ is linearly warmed up over 200 steps to reduce early posterior collapse. The VAE is trained for 3,000 updates at learning rate $10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Pixel DDPM}
The pixel denoiser is a two-resolution U-Net with residual time-conditioned blocks, sinusoidal timestep embeddings, long skip connections, and four-head bottleneck self-attention. The base width is 64 channels. Training lasts 5,000 updates at learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$. Sampling uses an EMA of the parameters with decay 0.995, exact posterior variance, and clips the predicted clean image to $[-1,1]$ before every posterior update. Inputs are mapped from $[0,1]$ to $[-1,1]$ before noising and mapped back to $[0,1]$ after sampling.

\subsection{Latent diffusion}
The latent denoiser uses the same U-Net design with base width 64 and four input/output channels. The VAE is first trained as above; its posterior mean is then standardized per latent channel. The denoiser is trained for 5,000 updates at $2\times10^{-4}$ with EMA decay 0.999. Sampling uses exact posterior variance and clips the predicted clean standardized latent to $[-3,3]$ before every posterior update. The reported LDM training cost includes both VAE and latent-denoiser training, and its parameter count includes the VAE and denoiser, because both components are required to generate an image. Sampling cost includes all 100 latent reverse steps and the final decoder pass.

\section{Evaluator and Hyperparameter Protocol}
\subsection{Frozen residual evaluator}
The evaluator is independent of all three generative models. It consists of three 64/128/256-channel residual stages and global average pooling, yielding a 256-dimensional feature before the classification layer. Residual connections are used to support deeper, trainable feature extractors \citep{he2016resnet}. A random horizontal flip is the only augmentation. Both evaluators are trained for 40 epochs with batch size 256, AdamW, and cosine decay. The selected learning rates are $3\times10^{-4}$ for Fashion-MNIST and $10^{-3}$ for the color dataset.

Four classifier configurations were compared on fixed stratified 90/10 splits of each training set. Selection maximized the mean of validation accuracy and validation balanced accuracy; the official test sets were not consulted. A square-root inverse-frequency loss was included as a class-imbalance ablation, motivated by class-balanced learning \citep{cui2019classbalanced}, but was not selected by the predefined score. Final evaluators are retrained on the complete training splits and then frozen. Table~\ref{tab:classifiers} and the confusion diagnostics expose their strengths and failure modes.

\subsection{Bounded generator search}
A small, reproducible search compares three VAE settings and four learning-rate/EMA combinations for each diffusion family. It uses seed 1508 and a class-balanced 1,000-image subset held out from Fashion-MNIST training; no official test image participates. The VAE pilot uses 1,500 updates, diffusion pilots use 2,000 updates, and candidates are ranked by task-feature FID. Random or bounded search is generally more efficient than exhaustive grid refinement when only a few hyperparameters dominate performance \citep{bergstra2012random}. The selected settings are transferred unchanged to the color dataset, preventing dataset-specific test tuning. Pilot scores are used only for selection; because short diffusion runs are not converged, they are not presented as definitive quality results.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
For each model--dataset--seed combination, 5,000 generated samples are evaluated. Fashion-MNIST uses 5,000 held-out real references; the smaller color dataset uses its complete 1,423-image test split. KID compares repeated equal-size 1,000-image subsets, while FID uses all available features in each set. Before every timed sampling pass, one untimed forward is run at each exact batch shape so cuDNN algorithm selection and kernel compilation do not affect seed 0 alone. Training still performs all configured optimizer updates, but wall-clock training excludes the first update, which serves the equivalent kernel warm-up purpose. CUDA is synchronized at every timing boundary.

\textbf{Distribution similarity.} FID compares the first two moments of real and generated feature distributions \citep{heusel2017fid}; KID is an unbiased polynomial-kernel maximum mean discrepancy estimator \citep{binkowski2018kid}. We compute both in the frozen classifier's 256-dimensional apparel feature space, not in ImageNet Inception space. We therefore call them \emph{feature FID} and \emph{feature KID}; absolute scores cannot be compared with published ImageNet FID. Metric sensitivity to feature extractors and sample treatment is a known limitation of generative evaluation \citep{stein2023flaws}.

\textbf{Recognizability and diversity.} We report mean and median evaluator confidence, the fraction with confidence at least 0.8, predicted-class coverage, normalized class entropy, Jensen--Shannon divergence (JSD) from the real predicted-class distribution, an Inception-style score, and mean pairwise feature distance. Coverage alone can hide strong mode imbalance; entropy and JSD make that failure visible.

\textbf{Structure and compute.} A true Sobel gradient-magnitude score provides a supporting edge diagnostic. Efficiency measurements include trainable parameters, peak allocated CUDA memory, measured training time, total sampling time, and amortized milliseconds per image at the specified large sampling batches. The latter is a throughput measurement, not single-request interactive latency. Timing is hardware- and implementation-dependent and should be interpreted on the reported RTX 5070 Ti environment.

\section{Numerical Results}
All central values below are means over seeds 0--4; uncertainty is the sample standard deviation. The test references and evaluator are fixed for all seeds within a dataset, while model initialization, data order, and stochastic training noise change with the seed.

\subsection{Evaluator reliability}
The Fashion-MNIST evaluator reaches 94.23\% test accuracy and 94.23\% balanced accuracy; its lowest per-class recall is 79.90\% for Shirt. This performance supports its use as a task-specific feature extractor while still exposing the known overlap between shirt-like categories. The color evaluator reaches 88.05\% overall accuracy but only 77.40\% balanced accuracy. Its weakest classes are Sweaters, Jackets, and Sweatshirts, with recalls of 31.82\%, 40.00\%, and 45.45\%, respectively. The 10.65-point gap between overall and balanced accuracy confirms that dominant categories drive part of the apparently strong aggregate score. Table~\ref{tab:classifiers}, Figure~\ref{fig:classifier-confusion}, and Figure~\ref{fig:classifier-recall} are therefore considered before interpreting feature metrics or predicted class proportions, especially for the imbalanced color set.

\section{Discussion}
The same distribution-quality ordering transfers across a balanced silhouette dataset and a smaller, imbalanced photographic dataset: DDPM leads, LDM is intermediate, and VAE trails. This agreement across every seed is stronger evidence than a favorable single run. The scale of the gaps is also practically substantial: DDPM cuts VAE FID by 75--80\%, while LDM cuts it by 56--60\%. Qualitative grids support this result. VAE outputs are frequently blurred or structurally ambiguous; DDPM preserves more texture, pose, and garment variation; LDM outputs are smooth and highly recognizable but repeat common silhouettes and dominant color-product categories more often.

The metrics do not produce a universal ranking. LDM has the highest classifier confidence, recognizability, and Inception-style score on both datasets, yet DDPM has substantially lower FID, KID, and class JSD. The LDM decoder removes some pixel-level noise and yields classifier-friendly prototypes, but its compressed 4$\times$8$\times$8 representation and stabilized latent range can suppress within-class detail and favor common modes. High confidence therefore measures semantic clarity, not distributional fidelity. The VAE provides the opposite extreme: its one-pass sampler is unmatched for throughput, but KL regularization and direct prior sampling yield the lowest confidence, feature spread, and class-proportion fidelity.

LDM is the strongest compromise when DDPM quality is desirable but pixel-space sampling cost is unacceptable. It gives up 43--50\% FID relative to DDPM, but reduces diffusion sampling time by about 92.8\% and peak allocated memory by roughly one third. Pixel DDPM is preferable when distribution match and class balance dominate the decision; VAE is preferable when rapid bulk generation or a small model matters more than fidelity. These conclusions describe the measured high-throughput implementation, not single-image service latency.

The experiment is intentionally a comparison of three complete systems. The models are neither parameter-matched nor training-FLOP-matched. Consequently, a difference cannot be attributed solely to the probabilistic objective: network width, representation size, number of sequential sampling steps, predicted-clean clipping, and the shared VAE component of the LDM all contribute. This system-level framing is appropriate for practical selection but weaker than a controlled architectural ablation.

The task-specific feature extractor is both a strength and a limitation. It is more relevant to low-resolution apparel than an off-the-shelf ImageNet network, but generator scores inherit its class confusion and any invariances learned during classifier training. This is most consequential for the color set, where 88.05\% overall accuracy coexists with 77.40\% balanced accuracy. The disclosed evaluator scores, confusion matrices, and per-class recall make this dependency auditable; qualitative grids and supporting diagnostics remain necessary.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
First, the bounded search cannot establish globally optimal hyperparameters; it identifies the best candidates under a stated compute budget. A broader asynchronous search could explore channel widths, latent channels, KL schedules, diffusion step counts, and learned variance. Second, all final diffusion samples use 100 ancestral steps. DDIM or modern solver-based samplers could reduce latency without retraining. Third, the LDM uses the same VAE objective as the standalone VAE; a perceptual or adversarial autoencoder objective could improve high-frequency reconstruction at the cost of a less controlled comparison. Fourth, the color dataset split is much smaller than Fashion-MNIST, and its 1,423 real references increase uncertainty in covariance-based metrics. Finally, wall-clock cost is specific to the tested software stack and RTX 5070 Ti.

Useful follow-up experiments include parameter-matched denoisers, equal-FLOP training budgets, confidence intervals from bootstrap resampling, precision/recall or density/coverage metrics, conditional generation, and a diffusion-step ablation. A second independent feature extractor would test whether the model ranking is robust to evaluator choice.

\section{Conclusion}
This project establishes a reproducible, five-seed comparison of a spatial VAE, pixel DDPM, and latent diffusion model across balanced grayscale and imbalanced color apparel domains. Under the frozen protocol, pixel DDPM consistently gives the best distribution match and class-proportion fidelity, with FID $29.64\pm1.49$ and $16.67\pm0.85$. LDM is the practical middle point: its FID is higher at $52.01\pm0.71$ and $33.65\pm2.21$, but it has the highest semantic recognizability and is 13.9 times faster to sample than DDPM. VAE generates approximately 292 times faster than DDPM but has the largest distribution error. The ranking is stable over all seeds, every evaluator-predicted class appears in every model group, and no run contains a non-finite loss. The evidence therefore supports application-specific selection: DDPM for fidelity, LDM for a quality--throughput compromise, and VAE for maximum throughput and minimum parameter count.
