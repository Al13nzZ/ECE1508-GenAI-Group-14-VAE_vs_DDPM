\begin{abstract}
We compare a spatial variational autoencoder (VAE), pixel-space denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM), and latent diffusion model (LDM) on balanced grayscale Fashion-MNIST and an imbalanced 15-class color-apparel subset. Each model--dataset pair is trained with five seeds, yielding 30 runs. A frozen residual classifier supplies task-specific features and class diagnostics. Pixel DDPM achieves the lowest feature FID on both datasets: $29.64\pm1.49$ and $16.67\pm0.85$, compared with LDM's $52.01\pm0.71$ and $33.65\pm2.21$, and VAE's $119.10\pm2.87$ and $83.88\pm3.80$. LDM samples 13.9 times faster than DDPM and gives the highest recognizability, while VAE is approximately 292 times faster than DDPM but has the largest distribution error. The results define a stable quality--compute frontier rather than a universally best model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Generative image models can represent the same data distribution through very different computational paths. A VAE regularizes a compact latent distribution and generates with one decoder pass \citep{kingma2014vae}. A DDPM learns to reverse gradual Gaussian corruption through sequential denoising \citep{ho2020ddpm}. An LDM performs this diffusion in a learned spatial latent and then decodes to pixels \citep{rombach2022ldm}. Their practical rankings may therefore depend on whether fidelity, distribution coverage, latency, or memory is most important.

This project asks two questions: (1) how do the three complete systems trade distribution quality against computational efficiency, and (2) does their ranking transfer from a balanced grayscale benchmark to a smaller, imbalanced photographic dataset? We use a common $32\times32$ RGB interface, 5,000 generated samples per trained model, five independent seeds, and a frozen task-specific evaluator. Hyperparameters are chosen using training-derived validation data only. The study is a system-level comparison, not a parameter- or FLOP-matched causal test of objectives.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
Let $x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)$ be an apparel image. We assume the standard VAE and diffusion formulations and state only the objectives needed to define the comparison. The VAE minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VAE}}=-\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\|\mathcal{N}(0,I)).
\end{equation}
Both diffusion systems use the cosine forward process \citep{nichol2021improved} and noise-prediction objective
\begin{equation}
x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,
\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{simple}}=\mathbb{E}\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Pixel DDPM applies the objective to $3\times32\times32$ images; LDM applies it to standardized $4\times8\times8$ latents and decodes only after denoising. Thus, LDM reduces each diffusion step from 1,024 to 64 spatial positions, while VAE removes iterative sampling entirely. We evaluate how these structural choices affect fidelity, semantic coverage, and measured resource cost on held-out data.

\section{Design}
\subsection{Datasets and controlled interface}
Fashion-MNIST provides 60,000 training and 10,000 test images in ten balanced classes \citep{xiao2017fashion}. Images are resized from $28\times28$ to $32\times32$ and replicated to RGB. The second dataset is Fashion Product Images Small \citep{fpis}. We retain the 15 most frequent Apparel article types and form a deterministic stratified 85/15 split: 8,007 training and 1,423 test photographs. Images are center-padded to a square before resizing, preserving aspect ratio. This set introduces natural class imbalance, color, texture, pose, and background variation.

\subsection{System configuration}
All three systems use residual convolutional blocks, GroupNorm, and SiLU. The diffusion U-Nets additionally use sinusoidal time embeddings, skip connections, bottleneck self-attention, and EMA sampling weights. The frozen evaluator has 64/128/256-channel residual stages and a 256-dimensional feature vector \citep{he2016resnet}. Table~\ref{tab:configuration} summarizes the settings that determine the principal capacity and sampling differences.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Optimization and sampling budgets}
All generators use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}, batch size 256, weight decay $10^{-4}$, gradient clipping at 1.0, 200-step linear warm-up, and cosine learning-rate decay \citep{loshchilov2017sgdr}. The VAE uses $\beta=1$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, and 3,000 updates. Each diffusion denoiser uses learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, 5,000 updates, and 100 ancestral reverse steps. DDPM uses EMA 0.995 and predicted-clean clipping to $[-1,1]$; LDM uses width 64, EMA 0.999, exact-posterior sampling, and clips standardized predicted-clean latents to $[-3,3]$. LDM training and parameter totals include its VAE.

Fixed update counts prevent the smaller color dataset from receiving a much smaller optimization budget merely because it contains fewer images; they do not equalize effective epochs. The common 100-step reverse budget isolates the effect of denoising representation while remaining computationally feasible. Per-channel latent standardization fixes the LDM signal scale presented to the cosine process; the $4\times8\times8$ representation has 12 times fewer scalars and 16 times fewer spatial positions than the pixel tensor.

\subsection{Validation-only selection}
Classifier settings are selected on fixed stratified 90/10 splits of each training set by the mean of accuracy and balanced accuracy. Generator learning-rate, KL-weight, and EMA candidates are selected on a balanced 1,000-image subset held out from Fashion-MNIST training. Full-budget validation then selects diffusion width, exact-posterior sampling, and predicted-clean clipping. These choices are material: the selected LDM configuration reduces held-out FID from 174.2 to 58.8 and restores sampled-latent SD from 13.3 to 0.842; exact-posterior sampling and image clipping reduce DDPM validation FID from 68.86 to 34.04. No official test image participates in selection. Appendix~\ref{app:tuning} contains the complete search and stabilization evidence. The bounded search identifies the best tested settings rather than a global optimum \citep{bergstra2012random}.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
The evaluator is trained only on real training images and frozen before generator evaluation. For each model--dataset--seed combination, we evaluate 5,000 generated samples against the same held-out real set. Feature FID compares Gaussian feature moments \citep{heusel2017fid}; KID is an unbiased polynomial-kernel MMD estimator \citep{binkowski2018kid}. Both use the evaluator's apparel features, so their absolute values are not ImageNet FID/KID and are comparable only within a dataset. We additionally measure confidence, confidence-$\geq0.8$ recognizability, classifier-based Inception Score (cIS), predicted-class coverage, normalized class entropy, JSD from the real predicted-class distribution, pairwise feature distance, Sobel gradient magnitude, training time, batched sampling time, parameters, and peak allocated CUDA memory. No single metric is treated as sufficient because all feature and label diagnostics inherit evaluator behavior \citep{stein2023flaws}. Results are descriptive mean$\pm$sample SD over five independently trained seeds, rather than significance claims from a large-sample study.

\section{Numerical Experiments}
\subsection{Implementation and evaluator reliability}
Experiments run in PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 12.8 on an RTX 5070 Ti. Native BF16, pinned-memory loaders, persistent workers, cuDNN benchmarking, and a 4,096-image diffusion sampling batch improve throughput. Exact-shape warm-ups and CUDA synchronization isolate steady-state timing. Every central value is a mean over seeds 0--4 and uncertainty is sample standard deviation.

The final Fashion-MNIST evaluator achieves 94.23\% accuracy and balanced accuracy; Shirt is the hardest class at 79.90\% recall. The color evaluator achieves 88.05\% accuracy but 77.40\% balanced accuracy, a 10.65-point gap; rare Sweaters, Jackets, and Sweatshirts have only 31.82\%, 40.00\%, and 45.45\% recall. Class weighting raises minimum validation recall from 25.00\% to 41.67\% but lowers the predefined combined selection score from 84.81\% to 84.60\%, so the unweighted evaluator is retained and both accuracy measures are disclosed. Generated-label metrics on the color set are therefore evaluator-relative rather than ground truth. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluator} reports the confusion matrices, per-class recall, and test-set imbalance.

\subsection{Recognizability, diversity, and sharpness}
LDM has the highest confidence and recognizability: it exceeds DDPM recognizability by 4.62 percentage points on Fashion-MNIST and 3.46 points on color products. Its smoother, classifier-friendly images therefore look semantically decisive even though DDPM better matches the complete real distribution. All systems cover all 10 or 15 evaluator-predicted classes in every seed, so coverage alone fails to distinguish them.

The quality--compute frontier in Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} is substantial. Across datasets, VAE requires 1.61--1.66 minutes to train and 0.036 ms/image to sample; LDM requires 3.48--3.78 minutes and 0.763--0.765 ms/image; DDPM requires 5.55--5.62 minutes and 10.611--10.624 ms/image. LDM is 13.9 times faster than DDPM and reduces its end-to-end training time by 33--37\%; VAE is about 292 times faster than DDPM and 21 times faster than LDM. These batched rates correspond approximately to 27,600, 1,310, and 94 images/s for VAE, LDM, and DDPM, not single-request latency.

Complete-system parameter counts are 1.955M, 7.743M, and 5.787M for VAE, LDM, and DDPM. Despite having 33.8\% more parameters than DDPM because its total includes the autoencoder, LDM has 32\% lower measured peak memory: 5.20--5.21 GB versus 7.68--7.70 GB. VAE peaks at 6.30--6.32 GB. This inversion shows that the 4,096-sample activation geometry, rather than weights alone, dominates high-throughput inference memory. All 30 runs have finite losses and no detected collapse. Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} provides convergence and separate training/sampling memory plots.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Model-selection implications}
DDPM is preferred when distribution fidelity and class-proportion agreement dominate: it leads FID, KID, and JSD in every seed. LDM is the best compromise when pixel diffusion is too expensive; it accepts 43--50\% higher FID than DDPM but removes 92.8\% of diffusion sampling time and leads recognizability and cIS. This disagreement shows that individually clear samples can still misrepresent global feature and class statistics. VAE is appropriate when speed and model size dominate, at a substantial fidelity cost. Because optimizer settings were selected on Fashion-MNIST and transferred unchanged, the persistent color-domain ranking demonstrates robustness without test-set tuning, not color-domain optimality.

\subsection{Robustness and limitations}
The systems are neither parameter- nor FLOP-matched, so conclusions concern complete implementations rather than objectives in isolation. Fixed updates also expose the 8,007-image color set more often than Fashion-MNIST. Its 1,423-reference test set and imperfect evaluator add uncertainty, while FID, KID, cIS, confidence, and feature distance reuse the same features; cross-dataset values are therefore not comparable. Timings measure RTX 5070 Ti throughput at large batch size, not interactive latency. Finally, the common 100-step ancestral sampler controls reverse depth but may miss each diffusion model's best frontier. Priority follow-ups are equal-compute comparisons, independent feature spaces, real-reference bootstrapping, and step-count/solver ablations.

\section{Conclusions}
Across both apparel domains, pixel DDPM gives the closest distribution, LDM the best quality--throughput compromise, and VAE the greatest speed and compactness. The stable five-seed ranking defines a use-dependent frontier; recognizability and feature fidelity can diverge. Future work should match compute, test faster samplers, strengthen the latent objective, and use independent features.
